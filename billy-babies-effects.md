# Billy Babies — Card Text Effect Ideas

This document proposes card text effects for the current [game rules](billy-babies-rules.md). These are design ideas, not additions to the agreed rules. Example numbers need playtesting.

## Design direction

Card text should create choices about which emotion lanes to contest, what to keep on the table, what to discard, and when to end the round. Effects should give cards distinct personalities beyond stronger dice.

Most effects should be short and conditional. A smaller number of unusual cards can change a player's priorities. Aim for cards that feel especially useful at the right moment rather than useful in every situation.

## Effect families

| Effect type | Example card text | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comebacks | “If you're behind in Sad, gain 4 Sad.” | Makes a card's value depend on the current board. |
| Redistributing emotions | “Move up to 5 of your points from Crazy to Peaceful.” | Turns an oversized lead into a chance at another checkmark. |
| Risky bargains | “You may lose 3 Joy to gain 6 Crazy. You must have at least 3 Joy.” | Creates deliberate sacrifices and supports different strategies. |
| Persistent support | “Once per turn, when you play another card with Love dice, gain 1 Peaceful.” | Creates a small engine to build around and a target for opposing removal. |
| Disrupting engines | “Remove an opposing card with persistent text and a magic value of 40 or less.” | Breaks combinations and reduces opposing table magic. Any quality points contributed by the removed card also reverse. |
| Protecting favorites | “The first time another friendly card would be removed by an opponent's card effect, remove this card instead.” | Creates bodyguards and decisions about which threat to target first. |
| Quality manipulation | “Move the Active Quality marker up to 3 points toward the center, stopping at zero.” | Buys time when the round is about to end before you're ready. |
| Hand selection | “Look at the top 3 cards of your draw pile. Put them back in any order.” | Helps plan upcoming turns without increasing hand size. If fewer than 3 cards remain, look at those available. |
| Discard recovery | “You may exchange one card in your hand with a card in your discard pile.” | Makes the mandatory discard a strategic resource and allows timely reuse. Requires a card in each location. |
| Wand preparation | “Gain 30 additional wand magic, up to the 400 cap.” | Helps threaten a cancellation sooner or save charge for the next round. |
| Table-magic rewards | “If your table magic is lower than your opponent's, gain 4 Shy.” | Gives smaller boards a way to compete with players stacking persistent cards. |
| Exhaustion resilience | “Once while this is on the table, reduce an empty-deck penalty's loss by 1 in each selected lane.” | Supports longer games without preventing exhaustion from ending the round. |

## Make the mandatory discard matter

Discard rewards can make the decision about which card to sacrifice part of a player's strategy. Examples of persistent text:

- “Once per turn, when you discard a card with Sad dice during your normal discard, gain 2 Joy.”
- “Once per turn, when you discard a card with the Active Quality during your normal discard, gain 15 wand magic, up to the 400 cap.”

A one-time effect can also reward a stocked discard pile:

- “When played, gain 1 Peaceful for each card in your discard pile, up to 5.”

Normal-discard triggers should not activate for canceled cards, removed table cards, or cards discarded after resolving. Recovery effects should be bounded so repeated reuse does not dominate play.

## More effect options

The following 40 options expand the pool. Unless an example describes a recurring trigger or duration, it resolves once during the card-text step. Targets and player choices follow the existing declaration rule. Emotion losses stop at zero, wand gains obey the 400 cap, and a round ending interrupts all remaining effects.

### Emotion tactics

| Option | Example card text | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Close the gap | “Choose an emotion. If you're behind in it by 3 or fewer points, gain 4 points in it.” | Rewards precise timing around a narrow deficit. |
| Spread the love | “Gain 1 point in each emotion where your score is greater than zero.” | Supports decks that invest in several lanes. |
| Gentle sabotage | “Your opponent loses up to 3 points in a chosen emotion.” | Threatens a close opposing lead without building your own score. |
| Borrowed feelings | “Transfer up to 2 points from your opponent's chosen emotion lane to your score in that same lane.” | Creates a small steal whose swing can flip a contested lane. Gain only the points actually removed. |

### Hand, discard, and draw-pile choices

| Option | Example card text | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Save for later | “Put a chosen card from your discard pile on the bottom of your draw pile.” | Saves a useful card for later while adding one card before the next empty-pile cycle. |
| Planned reunion | “Put a chosen card from your discard pile on top of your draw pile.” | Sets up the next successful draw without immediately replaying the card. |
| Blind exchange | “Exchange a chosen card in your hand with the top card of your draw pile. If your draw pile is empty, do nothing.” | Trades a known option for an unknown one without changing hand size or making a draw attempt. |
| Simple tastes | “Once per turn, when you discard a Common card during your normal discard, gain 2 Peaceful.” | Makes lower-rarity cards useful companions for a discard strategy. |
| Missing piece | “Choose an emotion. If no card in your hand has dice for that emotion, gain 3 points in it.” | Helps cover a gap in the remaining hand. Check after the played card has left the hand. |

Top-of-pile recovery is stronger than bottom-of-pile recovery and may enable repeated access to the same card. Both can delay exhaustion, so test them together with discard engines rather than balancing each in isolation.

### Wand decisions

| Option | Example card text | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spend the spark | “You may spend 50 wand magic to gain 4 Love.” | Trades progress toward a cancellation for emotion points. The player must have the full cost. |
| Out of charge | “If you have less than 200 wand magic, gain 3 Shy.” | Gives a card a different role after a wand has been spent. |
| Keep a reserve | “If you have at least 200 wand magic, gain 3 Peaceful.” | Rewards holding a cancellation in reserve. Evaluate before this card's normal wand gain. |
| Drain the battery | “Your opponent loses up to 25 wand magic.” | Can delay a future cancellation or reduce charge carried into the next round. It cannot undo a cancellation already used against this play. |
| Spare sparks | “While this is on the table, once per turn, if your normal wand-gain step would exceed 400, gain 2 Joy.” | Gives capped charge a modest side benefit. Wand magic still stops at 400. |

Small wand changes can cross the 200-magic cancellation threshold. Their practical value depends on that threshold, not just the number printed on the card. Avoid cheap recurring drain that prevents an opponent from ever using their wand.

### Persistent plans and countdowns

These proposals add start-of-turn triggers and visible counters. They need a timing rule before implementation. For these examples, start-of-turn effects happen before the play step, and only while the source remains on the table.

| Option | Example card text | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slow bloom | “At the start of your turn, gain 1 Love.” | Creates steady value if the opponent leaves the card in play. |
| Birthday countdown | “This enters the table with 2 counters. At the start of your turn, remove 1 counter. When the last is removed this way, gain 6 Joy.” | Advertises a delayed reward and gives the opponent time to answer. The reward happens once per table entry. |
| Growing courage | “Once per turn, when you play another card with Shy dice, add 1 counter to this card. When its third counter is added, gain 5 Crazy. This ability then stops triggering.” | Builds a visible goal around a particular emotion. |
| Quiet comeback | “At the start of your turn, if you're behind in at least 4 emotion lanes, gain 2 Sad and 2 Shy.” | Helps a struggling player remain competitive, with clear conditions the opponent can watch. |
| Recharging friend | “At the start of your turn, gain 10 wand magic.” | Makes table survival relevant to later cancellations and future rounds. |

### More experimental effects

These options need additional rules for information, temporary statuses, or choices during resolution. Treat them as a later testing pool.

| Option | Example card text | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Read the room | “Look at your opponent's hand.” | Helps plan around visible threats without taking a card away. Define when the reveal ends; later draws stay hidden. |
| Awkward offer | “Your opponent chooses: you gain 4 Joy, or they lose up to 4 Love.” | Lets the opponent choose the consequence they can best tolerate. This needs a choice during resolution, with a timer and timeout default. |
| Quiet time | “A chosen opposing table card's persistent text is inactive until the start of your next turn.” | Temporarily interrupts an engine while preserving that card's magic and existing quality contribution. |
| Blanket fort | “Choose an emotion. Until the start of your next turn, your score in that lane cannot be reduced by your opponent's card text.” | Protects a narrow lead for one opposing turn. It does not stop opposing gains or empty-deck penalties. |
| Comfort zone | “Choose an emotion as you play this. While this is on the table, reduce each empty-deck penalty's loss to your score in that lane by 2.” | Creates a protected lane for a longer-game strategy without changing failed draws or exhaustion counts. |

For transfers such as Borrowed feelings, protection must reduce the amount transferred as well as the amount lost; blocking the loss cannot create free points for the attacker.

## Effects that encourage deck synergies

These 28 proposals reward the other cards a player chooses, the resources those cards produce, or the actions they enable. The groups are potential strategies, not fixed deck lists. Several effects intentionally connect groups so players can build hybrids.

The strongest starting candidates, in my view, are emotion conversion, discard combinations, and sacrifice with recovery. Each can change both deck selection and turn-by-turn decisions. The other groups offer different pacing and resource priorities. None is a proven competitive deck yet.

### Conventions for these proposals

- Text that watches later events or provides a departure reward is persistent. Other text resolves once at the normal card-text step.
- “After resolving another card” means after that friendly card's normal resolution, including wand gain, if the round continues. The watching card must already have been on the table when the play began and still be there when its ability resolves. Canceled plays do not qualify.
- Dice watchers inspect final results after replacements or rerolls, once per resolved card. They do not reward both the original result and its replacement.
- “Sacrifice” is proposed shorthand for removing a friendly table card as explicitly instructed by your own card text. It follows the existing removal rules, including reversing quality contributions. Opposing removal, normal discards, cancellation, and clearing the board between rounds do not count. Existing effects such as Fond farewell and Fair trade would qualify for this proposed event.
- Event rewards wait until the effect that caused them has finished, and require their source to remain on the table. An explicit “when this card is sacrificed” ability is an exception: record it as the card leaves, then resolve it if the round continues. Endpoints always stop resolution before pending rewards.
- Choices are declared before the cancellation window. An emotion chosen for a persistent ability stays fixed for that table entry. Costs must be paid in full, point transfers move only existing points, and wand gains stop at 400.
- “Once per turn” applies separately to each source card. “Once per round” follows the individual card even if it leaves and returns. Track the round history needed by an effect openly and reset it between rounds.

These conventions are proposals for evaluating the examples; they do not change the agreed rules. Ordering several pending triggers still needs a shared procedure before implementation.

### 1. Emotion specialists that can contest other lanes

A focused deck needs ways to turn its preferred emotion into more than one checkmark. These effects reward specialization while creating routes into other lanes.

| ID / option | Example card text | Cards it encourages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SYN-01 — Joy to spare | “At the start of your turn, if you lead Joy by at least 5, move 2 of your Joy points to Shy.” | Joy producers and effects that protect a lead. Excess Joy becomes another contested lane. |
| SYN-02 — Shared sadness | “Once per turn, after resolving another card with Sad dice, gain 1 Love and 1 Peaceful.” | A variety of Sad cards, including ones otherwise too narrow to fit a broad scoring deck. |
| SYN-03 — Best friends | “Choose two different emotions. Once per turn, after resolving another card with dice for both, gain 1 point in each chosen emotion.” | Cards sharing a particular emotion pair. The player chooses the pair to fit their deck. |
| SYN-04 — Feelings in motion | “Once per turn, after another friendly card's text moves points between two of your emotion lanes, gain 2 extra points in the destination lane.” | Redistribution effects and Joy to spare. Ordinary point gains and steals from the opponent do not trigger this. |

**Example connection:** Joy producers feed Joy to spare; Feelings in motion makes its Shy transfer more rewarding. The player can also include one-time redistribution cards, so the plan does not require that exact pair. Its weakness is needing enough points and time to establish the initial lead.

### 2. Discard combinations and useful returns

These effects make a card's relationship to the rest of the hand matter, and reward recovery without granting extra plays.

| ID / option | Example card text | Cards it encourages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SYN-05 — Parting harmony | “Once per turn, when you make your normal discard, if that card shares an emotion on its printed dice with the card you successfully played this turn, gain 2 Crazy.” | Overlapping emotion profiles and hand selection. The played and discarded cards must share at least one emotion. |
| SYN-06 — Welcome back | “Once per turn, after another friendly card's text moves a card from your discard pile into your hand, gain 2 Shy.” | Discard exchanges and sacrifice effects that recover a card. Normal draws and reshuffles do not qualify. |
| SYN-07 — Familiar face | “Choose an emotion, a card in your hand with dice for it, and a card in your discard pile with dice for it. You may exchange those cards. If you do, gain 2 points in that emotion.” | Emotion-focused decks with several reusable cards. Both exchange targets must exist when declared. |
| SYN-08 — A little of everything | “Once per turn, when you discard a card with no persistent text during your normal discard, gain 1 point in each emotion printed on that card's dice.” | Broad dice profiles and cards that can serve either as immediate plays or valuable discards. |

**Example connection:** Familiar face activates Welcome back while shaping the hand for this turn's discard or a later play. Parting harmony rewards maintaining enough overlapping emotion profiles to pair a successful play with that turn's discard. These combinations compete for the same small hand, so choosing what to recover and what to keep remains meaningful.

### 3. Sacrifice with a reason to rebuild

This group adds cards worth sacrificing, rewards for doing so, and ways to recover a different useful card.

| ID / option | Example card text | Cards it encourages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SYN-09 — A proper send-off | “Once per turn, after you sacrifice another friendly card, gain 2 Joy and 2 Sad.” | Several distinct sacrifice effects rather than a single indispensable removal card. |
| SYN-10 — Parting gift | “When this card is sacrificed, gain 3 Peaceful.” | Fond farewell, Fair trade, and other sacrifice effects. Its future departure ability keeps it on the table after a normal play. |
| SYN-11 — Pass it on | “You may sacrifice a chosen friendly table card. If you do, return a chosen card already in your discard pile to your hand.” | Expendable table cards, departure rewards, and Welcome back. Choose both targets before the cancellation window; the recovered card cannot be the sacrificed card. |
| SYN-12 — Remember the family | “If you have sacrificed at least 2 different cards this round, gain 3 Joy and 3 Sad.” | A deck with multiple sacrifice targets and ways to remove them. Repeatedly sacrificing one card does not meet the condition. |

**Example connection:** Pass it on can sacrifice Parting gift, recover a previous play, and activate A proper send-off. This costs a table card and its magic; any quality reversal can end the round before the recovery or rewards occur. Test the combined reward rather than pricing each effect alone.

| SYN-13 — Everyone has bad days | “Once per turn, after resolving another card, if any of its emotion dice finished on 1, gain 2 Peaceful.” | Normal and Strong dice. Normal dice have two faces showing 1; Brutal dice have none. |

### 7. Quality pressure and surviving a longer round

These options connect a deck's scoring plan to its preferred round length. They use the current Active Quality rather than requiring one specific quality to be selected before the match.

| ID / option | Example card text | Cards it encourages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SYN-25 — Quality time together | “Choose two different emotions. Once per turn, after resolving another card with the Active Quality, gain 1 point in each chosen emotion.” | Cards covering different possible qualities, with chosen emotion rewards filling gaps in the deck. |
| SYN-26 — Closing chorus | “Once per turn, after resolving another card without the Active Quality, if you lead at least 3 emotion lanes, move the quality marker 1 point toward your side.” | Broad scoring and off-quality plays that help close a round after establishing emotion leads. Attribute this movement to the watching card for later reversal. |
| SYN-27 — Pick each other up | “Once per turn, after an empty-deck penalty actually reduces any of your emotion scores, gain 2 Love.” | Long-lived table engines and partial penalty protection. A penalty causing no actual loss grants nothing. |
| SYN-28 — Weather the wait | “At the start of your turn, if you have exhausted at least once this round and have at least 2 other friendly table cards with Peaceful dice, gain 2 Shy and 2 Sad.” | Persistent Peaceful cards, protection, and ways to keep the quality track away from an endpoint. |

**Example connection:** Peaceful support cards can survive into the exhaustion phase, where Weather the wait helps contest two additional lanes. Pick each other up softens actual losses. Closing chorus gives an opposing scoring deck a potential way to end the round through quality before the long-game rewards accumulate.

### Turn these proposals into different deck choices

- Give each strategy several distinct enabling cards and rewards, including Common and Uncommon options. A 20-card singleton deck should still function when one important card is discarded or canceled.
- Include cards with more than one useful connection. Familiar face can serve emotion specialists or discard decks; Pass it on can serve sacrifice or recovery decks; Light up the family can serve table-building or wand-spending decks.
- Match recurring rewards with an appropriate cost in initial dice, magic, setup, or vulnerability. Persistent text also keeps a card's magic on the table, so its ongoing reward is not its only benefit.
- Watch stacking across different cards. A once-per-turn limit on each source does not cap the combined reward from several sources observing the same event.
- Preserve distinctions between gains, transfers, sacrifices, discards, recovery, and draws. This keeps one event from accidentally activating every strategy or creating a self-sustaining loop.
- Build prototype decks and test which cards they choose, which they discard, when they spend their wand, and when they try to end a round. These are the measures of strategic variety; the number of effect names alone is not evidence of it.

## Let players influence when the round ends

Reaching a quality endpoint ends the round but does not automatically win it. A quality push can finish a round while a player leads in checkmarks, or end it too early while that player is behind.

Effects that move the marker toward the center offer a way to buy time. Effects that push it toward a player can create finishing opportunities. Their value depends on the emotion lanes and table-magic tiebreaker as well as the track itself.

Removal is particularly consequential under the current rules. It can stop a persistent effect, lower table magic, reverse the removed card's quality contribution, and potentially end the round. Removal strength should account for all of these benefits.

## Timing and wording to settle before implementation

### Follow the existing resolution order

The current order is emotion dice, matching Active Quality points, card text, table placement, then wand gain. Targets and printed choices are declared before the wand cancellation window. A canceled card produces no effects.

Conditions such as “if you're behind in Sad” or “if your table magic is lower” should be evaluated when the text resolves. The played card has not yet entered the table at that point.

### Round endings interrupt effects

Reaching a quality endpoint ends resolution immediately. Text cannot rescue a play that already reached an endpoint through its printed quality points. Removal that reverses quality points to an endpoint also ends the round immediately.

An exhaustion-resilience effect changes a penalty's point loss, not exhaustion counting. It does not apply when the triggering failed draw ends the round before a penalty occurs.

### Dice changes need explicit timing

Since text resolves after dice, a reroll effect needs wording such as: “Reroll one emotion die rolled by this card and replace its original result.” The score must be adjusted by the difference, rather than adding both results.

Choosing a die after seeing the roll would need an explicit exception to the current rule that printed choices are declared before the cancellation window. Alternatively, the effect can identify its die automatically, such as the lowest-result die, with a defined tie procedure.

### Track movement needs an ownership rule

Text that moves the quality marker must specify whether that movement counts as the card's reversible quality contribution. This is particularly important for movement toward the center and recurring movement.

Also clarify whether quality movement from text alone causes a card to remain on the table. The existing rules explicitly keep cards that supply quality points or have persistent text.

### Persistent triggers need a shared procedure

Define when “when you play” effects resolve relative to the played card's own effects, and how multiple simultaneous triggers are ordered. Recommended starting point: only successful, uncanceled plays activate these triggers.

Protection effects need a defined replacement procedure. Prevent multiple protectors from repeatedly redirecting the same removal. Once a replacement is selected, resolve it and check for an immediate round ending.

Use once-per-turn limits for recurring rewards where appropriate. Clarify whether a “once while on the table” ability resets when a card leaves and returns; the natural reading is that it does.

### Counters and temporary effects need visible lifetimes

For the countdown proposals, counters belong to the table card and clear when it leaves. Returning the card creates a fresh instance. Show remaining counters and spent abilities to both players.

Temporary effects need an explicit expiry event and a rule for what happens if their source leaves first. A useful starting convention is that “while this is on the table” effects end with their source, while an already-created “until the start of your next turn” effect lasts until that deadline. All such effects clear between rounds.

Start-of-turn triggers require a defined order when several are eligible. Check for a round ending after each resolved effect, just as during card resolution. Clearing the table between rounds should not activate departure rewards.

### Keep disruption bounded

Prefer modest point losses, limited wand drain, and short interruptions over repeated denial of turns or actions. The play-one/discard-one rhythm should remain the default experience.

Define how multiple loss reductions combine, with a floor of zero. A silence effect should stop future persistent triggers while active; it should not reverse previously earned emotion points, alter magic stats, or reverse quality contributions.

### Keep resource language precise

- Distinguish wand magic from table magic in every effect.
- Apply the existing 400 cap to wand gains.
- Use the card's match-frozen adjusted magic value for effects that compare magic stats, unless a different rule is explicitly adopted.
- Moving emotion points cannot move more points than the source lane contains.
- Looking at or reordering cards is not drawing and should not trigger failed-draw penalties.
- Keep hand exchanges separate from drawing so their interaction with exhaustion is clear.

## Recommended first set

Prioritize emotion redistribution, discard rewards, small persistent combinations, and targeted removal. These make the existing rules interact in interesting ways without introducing many new systems.

Add quality manipulation, protection, recovery, and exhaustion resilience once their timing is defined and the simpler effects have been tested. Evaluate effects for the decisions they create, not only their average point value.
